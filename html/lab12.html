WHO

The World Health Organization (WHO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations which coordinates responses to international public health issues and emergencies. It is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, and has six regional offices and 150 field offices worldwide. Only sovereign states are eligible to join, and it is the largest intergovernmental health organization at the international level.

The main purpose of the World Health Organization (WHO) is the attainment by all peoples of the highest possible level of health.


The emblem of the World Health Organization (WHO) was chosen in 1948

Headquarters: Geneva, Switzerland
Founded: April 7, 1948
Founders: United States, United Kingdom, France, Brazil
Parent organization: United Nations
